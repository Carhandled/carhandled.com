Your Pre-Approval Is
Your Leverage.

Why a rate from your bank or credit union is the strongest card you can bring to a dealer, when to show it, and the exact words to make them beat it.

By Ali Camur · — · Updated November 2026
On this page
Why it works How to get one When to show it What to say 0% vs the rebate Checklist FAQ

Most buyers walk into the finance office with no idea what interest rate they qualify for. That's the moment the dealer controls. A pre-approval from your own bank or credit union changes it: you walk in with a real number in writing, and the dealer has to beat it to earn your loan.

Why a pre-approval works

When a dealer arranges your loan, the lender approves you at a buy rate. The dealer can write your contract at a higher rate and keep part of the difference. Lenders often cap that markup at around 2 to 2.5 percentage points, and on a $30,000 loan over 60 months, each point costs you roughly $800.

A pre-approval puts a ceiling on that. If the dealer can't beat your number, you use your own lender. If they can, you take their rate. Either way, you stop guessing.

The short version

A pre-approval doesn't commit you to anything. It's a number the dealer has to beat.

How to get one

  • Start with a credit union. They often have the lowest auto rates, and membership is usually easy to qualify for.
  • Add your own bank for a second number.
  • Shop in a tight window. Several auto loan inquiries within a short period are usually scored as one. Keep it within about 14 days to be safe.
  • Get it in writing: rate, term, maximum amount and how long the offer is valid, usually 30 to 60 days.
  • Ask about prepayment penalties so you know you can refinance or pay early.

When to show it

Use it as a bar to beat, not as your budget. Mention the rate and term early, in your first email to the dealer, but keep the price conversation about the out the door total, as if you were paying cash. That stops the dealer from trading a lower rate for a higher price.

Never share the maximum amount you were approved for. That's a budget, and budgets get spent.

What to say

In your first email

"I'm pre-approved at [X]% for [Y] months. If you can beat that, I'll finance with you. Please send the full out the door price first."

In the finance office

"What's the buy rate on this approval? My credit union is at [X]%. If you can't beat it, I'll use mine."

0% financing vs the cash rebate

Manufacturers often make you choose: a special rate or a cash rebate, rarely both. This is where your pre-approval does real math for you.

Example on a $30,000 loan over 60 months: 0% costs nothing in interest. A $2,500 rebate with a 6% pre-approval means you borrow $27,500, and the interest comes to about $4,400. In this case the 0% wins by roughly $1,900. With a bigger rebate, a lower rate or a shorter term, the answer can flip, so run both before you sign.

Checklist

  • Two pre-approvals in writing, within about 14 days
  • Rate and term mentioned in your first dealer email
  • Price negotiated as a cash out the door total
  • Maximum approval amount kept to yourself
  • Buy rate asked for in the finance office
  • 0% vs rebate compared on total cost
  • No prepayment penalty on whichever loan you take
