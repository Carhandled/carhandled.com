Comparing Offers

Compare Dealer Quotes
Line by Line.

You have a few written quotes. Here's how to check that they cover the same car, line them up on the out the door total, and pick the best offer with confidence.

By Ali · — · Updated November 2026
On this page

Getting written quotes from a few dealers is the hard part, and you've done it. Now you have three or four emails that all look a little different: one leads with a discount off MSRP, one lists a monthly payment, one has a package you didn't ask about. After more than 1,000 deals on the dealership side, I've seen plenty of buyers pick the wrong quote simply because the quotes weren't laid out the same way. This guide shows you how to compare them fairly.

Step 1: Make sure it's the same car

Before you look at a single dollar, confirm every quote is for an equivalent vehicle. Two cars with the same model name can be a few thousand dollars apart in equipment.

  • Year, trim and drivetrain. An AWD version or a higher trim changes the price on its own.
  • Packages and options. Ask for the window sticker or a build sheet so you can see what's on the car.
  • VIN or stock number. A quote on a specific car on the lot is different from a quote on a car that's still being built or in transit.
  • Miles. A new car used as a loaner or demo can have a few thousand miles on it, which should be reflected in the price.

If one dealer quoted a different trim, ask them to requote the closest match, or set that quote aside.

Step 2: Compare the out the door total

The number that matters is the out the door (OTD) price: everything you'll pay to drive the car home, including the selling price, the doc fee, any dealer-installed items, sales tax, and title and registration. If you need a refresher on what belongs in it, see what is out the door price.

Discounts off MSRP and monthly payments are useful details, but they leave things out. The OTD total is the one figure that puts every quote on equal footing.

If a quote doesn't show an OTD total yet, the OTD email guide has a short, friendly template to request one.

Step 3: Line up each item

Put the quotes side by side in a spreadsheet or on paper, with one row for each of these:

  1. Selling price of the vehicle before fees.
  2. Doc fee. Most dealers charge one for preparing the paperwork. The amount varies by dealer and, in some states, is limited by law.
  3. Dealer-installed items or packages, such as protection packages, tint or accessories. Note which ones are already on the car and which are optional.
  4. Rebates and incentives, and whether each one applies to you (some require financing with the manufacturer's lender, or a specific loyalty status).
  5. Sales tax. Tax rates are set by the government, not the dealer, so for the same car and the same buyer the tax should be close across quotes. A big gap usually means one dealer estimated differently. Just ask how they calculated it.
  6. Title, registration and government fees. Same idea as tax: these should be similar across quotes.
  7. Trade-in value, if you have one. Keep it on its own row so it doesn't blur the price of the new car.

Most of the variation will sit in the selling price, the doc fee and the dealer-installed items.

A worked example

Here are two quotes for the same car. Both use an example sales tax of 7% and $350 in title and registration. Your real tax and fees depend on where you live.

Dealer A
Selling price$34,200.00
Doc fee$499.00
Protection package$995.00
Sales tax (7%)$2,498.58
Title and registration$350.00
Out the door$38,542.58
Dealer B
Selling price$34,900.00
Doc fee$199.00
Sales tax (7%)$2,456.93
Title and registration$350.00
Out the door$37,905.93

Dealer A has the lower selling price by $700, but Dealer B's out the door total is $636.65 lower. If you only compared selling prices, you'd pick the more expensive deal.

Now suppose you ask Dealer A whether the protection package is optional, and they're happy to remove it. Their total drops to $37,477.93, which is $428.00 below Dealer B. One polite question changed the answer.

The short version

Same car, same rows, same tax assumptions. Then compare the bottom line.

What to ask when something doesn't line up

Most gaps clear up with a short, friendly email, and keeping it in writing means you both have the same numbers.

When a line is missing or unclear

"Thanks for the quote. To compare it with the others I have, could you send the full out the door total with each line listed: selling price, doc fee, any dealer-installed items, tax, and title and registration?"

When a package is included

"I see a $995 protection package on the quote. Is that already on the car, or is it optional? If it's optional, could you send the total without it?"

When the tax looks different

"Your tax estimate is a bit different from the other quotes I have. Could you let me know what rate and amount you used? I'll be registering the car in [city, state]."

If an add-on is already installed and can't come off, that's fine. Just count it in the total. For a closer look at which products can be worth it, see dealer add-ons: what to buy, what to skip.

Why monthly payments are hard to compare

A payment depends on the price, the down payment, the rate and the length of the loan. Change any one of them and the payment moves. For example, $500 a month for 72 months adds up to $36,000, while $560 a month for 60 months adds up to $33,600. The lower payment costs $2,400 more in total payments.

So compare the OTD totals first. Then, if you're financing, compare the rate and term separately. A pre-approval from your bank or credit union gives you a rate to measure the dealer's offer against.

Making the choice

The lowest total is usually the one to pick, but a few other things are worth a moment:

  • Availability. A car on the lot today is worth more to most people than one arriving in two months.
  • Expiration. Ask how long each quote is good for, since incentives can change at the end of the month.
  • Distance. If two totals are close, a dealer near home is handy for future service visits.

If you'd prefer to buy from a particular dealer, it's fair to share the best written total you have and ask if they'd like to earn your business. Good dealers appreciate a clear, prepared customer.

Thanking the dealers you didn't pick

"Thank you for the time you put into the quote. I've decided to go with another offer this time, but I appreciated how clear and quick you were."

Checklist

  • Every quote is for the same year, trim, packages and condition
  • Every quote shows a full out the door total
  • Selling price, doc fee, dealer items, tax and fees on separate rows
  • Rebates checked to make sure you qualify for each one
  • Trade-in kept on its own line
  • Optional items confirmed in writing
  • Rate and term compared separately from the price
  • Expiration date noted for each quote
