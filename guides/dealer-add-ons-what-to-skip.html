The Finance Office

Dealer Add-Ons:
What to Buy, What to Skip.

Every product the finance office will offer you, which ones can be worth it, which ones are pure margin, and the exact words to decline them.

By Ali · — · Updated November 2026
On this page
Why the finance office exists Sometimes worth it Always skip The rate markup What to say Cheat sheet FAQ

You've agreed on a price, you've signed the buyer's order, and you think you're done. Then you're walked into a small office for "paperwork." This is where a clean deal picks up $2,000 to $5,000 in products you didn't plan to buy. Everything offered in that room is optional, and some of it is worth less than a tenth of what you'll be quoted.

Why the finance office exists

The finance and insurance (F&I) manager is a salesperson. They're paid mostly on back end gross: the products they sell and the markup on your interest rate. They're also measured on penetration, the share of buyers who leave with a service contract, GAP and the rest. That's why the pitch continues after you say no, and why "almost everyone takes this one" is said in every store in the country.

None of that makes the products scams. A few can be genuinely useful. But you should decide on them the way you'd decide on anything else you buy: with a price, the terms in writing, and time to compare.

The first rule

Everything in the finance office is optional. Nothing is "standard," "required by the bank," or "already included" unless you agreed to it in writing before you walked in.

Sometimes worth it

GAP insurance

Covers the difference between what you owe and what the car is worth if it's totaled or stolen. Buy it if you put less than 20% down or finance for 72 months or more. Skip the dealer's version if your auto insurer or credit union offers it, which is usually far cheaper.

Say: "What's the price? I'll compare it with my insurance company."

Extended warranty (vehicle service contract)

Covers repairs after the factory warranty ends. Worth considering if you'll keep the car well past the factory warranty and the model has a weak reliability record. Skip it if you'll sell within five years. Most can be bought later, closer to when the factory warranty runs out, and the price is negotiable.

Say: "Can I buy this closer to when my factory warranty expires?"

Prepaid maintenance

Prepays oil changes and scheduled service at dealer prices. Worth it only if it costs less than paying as you go and is refundable pro rata if you sell or total the car. Skip it if you use an independent shop.

Say: "Is this fully refundable pro rata if I sell or total the car?"

Tire and wheel protection

Covers road hazard damage to tires and wheels. Can make sense on low profile tires or rough roads, where one bent wheel costs more than the plan. Skip it on standard tires and decent roads.

Say: "Show me the deductible and the claim process in writing."

Always skip at dealer prices

Paint sealant, fabric protection, ceramic coating

Often marked up 400% to 600%. Better products are available later for a fraction of the price, applied by a detailer you choose.

VIN etching, nitrogen fill, GPS tracking

VIN etching runs $200 to $400 at the dealer and about $30 as a kit at an auto parts store. Air is already 78% nitrogen. If these are "already on the car," ask for them to be removed or for the full cost to come off the selling price.

Say: "Remove it, or take the full cost off the selling price."

The markup you can't see: your interest rate

When the dealer arranges your loan, the lender approves you at a buy rate. Your contract can be written at a higher rate, and the dealer keeps part of the difference. It's legal and it's in the paperwork, but it rarely gets mentioned. On a $30,000, 60 month loan, one extra percentage point costs you roughly $800 over the life of the loan.

The fix is simple: walk in with a pre-approval from your bank or credit union. Then the dealer has to beat a real number to earn your loan. We explain why that works in what an out-the-door price includes, and how to get the number in writing in the OTD email guide.

Say: "What's the buy rate? My credit union approved me at [X]%."

Here is how to get a pre-approval and use it as leverage.

What to say when you sit down

One sentence

"I'm declining all products today. Please print the contract with financing only." You don't owe an explanation. If the pitch continues, repeat it once and ask for the paperwork.

Before you sign, check three things against your buyer's order: the OTD total, the interest rate and the term. If any of them changed, or a product appeared that you didn't agree to, stop and ask for the manager. A deposit is not a contract, and you can still leave.

Cheat sheet

  • GAP: sometimes, usually cheaper from your insurer
  • Extended warranty: sometimes, and you can usually buy it later
  • Prepaid maintenance: sometimes, only if refundable and cheaper
  • Tire and wheel: sometimes, low profile tires only
  • Paint, fabric, ceramic: skip
  • VIN etch, nitrogen, GPS: skip
  • Rate markup: beat it with a pre-approval
